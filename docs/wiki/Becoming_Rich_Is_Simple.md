## Renting vs. Owning a Home

- Use index funds. Your brain works against long-term decisions because it’s designed for immediate survival.
- **Best approach:** don’t look at your investments often. The more people look, the less risk they take, and the lower the returns.
- You don’t need extensive background knowledge to start.
-Young/saving: it can be suboptimal for young people to save aggressively. Save more when your income is higher, less when it isn’t. Watch out for lifestyle inflation — if you spend every dollar you earn, you won’t have the savings habit when income rises.

## Top 10 Financial Mistakes

These are the mistakes that quietly keep people broke, per Ben Felix and David Bach.

1. **Not earning enough money** — People get stuck thinking they can’t increase income. Improve your knowledge and skills; that improves your human capital.
2. **Not saving enough** — Wealth compounds over time. Pay yourself first: e.g., direct 12% of income into pre-tax deductible retirement savings (RRSP).
3. **Not setting financial goals** — You can work for years toward things that don’t matter to you. Either you have a plan for your money or someone else will.
4. **Overspending on the wrong things** — Your spending should reflect your values. Write your values, write your expenses, check for alignment.
5. **Not taking investment risks** — Not participating in the stock market gives up enormous long-term gain.
6. **Taking the wrong risks** — Not all risk is equal; smart risk-taking matters.
7. **Missing tax-planning opportunities** — RRSP, TFSA, estate planning.
8. **Marrying the wrong person** — Partner financial habits directly impact future wealth.
9. **Underinsuring catastrophic risks** — Many people are one bad event away from disaster.
10. **No estate plan** — Everyone has a default government will; you may not like it. Anyone with dependents should have a will.

Average age of widowhood is **59**. If your spouse died tomorrow, would you know where everything is?

## David Bach's Money Rules

Core philosophy: when you are clear on your values, the decision-making process around your money becomes easier.

- Your phone is either making you money or costing you money. It is arguably the greatest technology ever created to separate you from your wealth.
- “Lifetime value of a customer” thinking: subscription model = ongoing value.
- Most people have a **“no-plan plan.”**

### Have a Plan

Allocate income into buckets:

1. **Future you** — retirement investing
2. **Emergency fund** — 3–6 months of expenses in a liquid money-market account
3. **Dream fund** — goals between now and retirement

The faster you fund your dreams, the more time you have for compounding to work.

**Time horizon guidance:**
- 1–2 years: money market
- 5 years: balanced fund
- Longer: more aggressive allocation

**Employer plan transitions:**
- Roll 401K into an IRA or new employer’s plan when changing jobs.
- Ensure the transfer is *actually invested* (transfers often land as cash).
- Set your contribution rate; many employers default to 3%, not 12%.
- Set minimums to auto-pay on all debts to avoid late fees.
- Pay off the smallest balance first for psychological momentum.

### Have a Money Date

Regularly review expenses, income, debts, and goals.

### First Three Steps for Investing

1. List your goals.
2. **Double the list** — this forces you to think harder about what matters.

Align goals with the **PERMA model**:
- **P**ositive Emotion
- **E**ngagement
- **R**elationships
- **M**eaning (something bigger than yourself)
- **A**ccomplishment (setting goals and achieving them)

### 100-Day Savings Challenge

Save a fixed amount daily for 100 days. A jar works fine — visible progress is motivating.

> Save $20/day for 40 years → ~$4,000,000.

## Unrecoverable Costs of Owning a Home

- Mortgage interest
- Opportunity cost of down payment
- House equity rises at about the rate of inflation
- Property taxes
- Maintenance costs (~1–2% of property value per year)
- Time spent maintaining / coordinating maintenance
- Emergency costs (roof, foundation, appliances) — require liquidity, adding more opportunity cost
- Renovation spending

**Total carrying cost:** roughly **5% of house value per year**.

## Side-by-Side: Rent vs. Own

- Rent from professional landlords only when total cost of ownership exceeds the carrying cost.
- Compare:
  - Cash-flow cost difference
  - Expected rent increases vs. expected equity increase
  - Opportunity costs
- Stocks are taxed; home capital gains may not be.
- Owning limits mobility — negative equity can trap you.
- Transaction costs when changing houses.
- Life needs change over time.

**Example:**  
300K house → 5%/12 = ~$1,250/month is the break-even cost vs. equivalent investment.

## Lifecycle Asset Allocation

**Optimal portfolio for retirement:**
- 100% equity
- 2/3 international, 1/3 domestic

Studies tested:
- Static balanced portfolios
- Target-date funds (shifts equity/bond ratio with age)
- 100% equity portfolios

**Findings:**
- Bonds get decimated during high-inflation periods.
- Stocks are less risky than assumed; bonds are more risky than assumed.

## Things NOT to Invest In

- **Covered calls** — Plays on the income bias; usually underperform buy-and-hold.
- **Cash** — Inflation erodes purchasing power over time.
- **Thematic ETFs** (Space, AI, etc.) — Often launched at cycle highs, then drift lower as interest rates rise.
- **Bitcoin / speculative crypto** — Speculative asset, not an investment.
- Over-hyped products that sound complex but under-deliver.

## Retirement Planning

Ties into the **PERMA model** — retirement should fund the life you want to live.

- People eventually reach a point where they can’t work any more.
- Some choose to keep working in retirement for fulfillment and purpose.
- The goal is financial design that supports your preferred lifestyle.

## Efficient Market Hypothesis

A perfectly efficient market means prices always fully reflect all available information.

If markets are efficient:
- The future is already priced in.
- You are investing in discounted future cash flows.

## Professional Money Managers

- **Most professional managers do NOT beat the market.**
- Those who beat it in one period typically do not sustain outperformance long-term.
- Indexing is often the rational default for most investors.
