# CFI M&A Process — 10-Step Workflow

> Aligned with **Corporate Finance Institute (CFI)** M&A Process framework.
> Reference: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/ma-process/
>
> Supplement to the DCF methodology in `cfi-dcf-methodology.md`.

---

## Overview

Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A) is the process by which two companies combine to create
value through strategic synergies, market expansion, or operational consolidation. The
CFI framework defines a 10-step structured workflow from initial strategy through
post-close integration.

---

## The 10-Step M&A Process (CFI)

### Step 1 — Develop a Strategic Rationale

Before any deal activity, the acquirer must define why the target adds value:

- **Horizontal integration**: acquire a direct competitor to increase market share
- **Vertical integration**: upstream (supplier) or downstream (distribution)
- **Market extension**: enter new geographies or customer segments
- **Diversification**: reduce revenue concentration risk
- **Acqui-hire**: acquire for talent (intangible asset)

**Output**: Acquisition thesis with explicit synergy targets (e.g. $50M annual cost savings).

---

### Step 2 — Identify and Screen Targets

Build a target universe using:

- Financial screening (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, CAGR bands)
- Product/technology alignment (capability gap analysis)
- Geography and regulatory fit
- Ownership structure (public, private, divisions of larger companies)

Create a **Long List** → narrow to a **Short List** of 5–10 candidates.

---

### Step 3 — Outreach and Initial Contact

- Direct contact with target management or board
- Engagement of **investment bankers** to run a sell-side or buy-side mandate
- **Solicited vs. unsolicited bids**: unsolicited requires more persuasion and public的压力

Deliverables: Confidentiality Agreement (NDA), teaser or corporate summary.

---

### Step 4 — NDA and Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM)

Once NDA is in place, the seller (via investment bank) distributes:

- **CIM**: 50–200 page document with company overview, financials, growth strategy,
  market positioning, and risk factors.
- **Data Room**: virtual data room (VDR) with contracts, leases, litigation, cap table,
  customer contracts, and detailed financial schedules.

Review CIM and flag key diligence areas.

---

### Step 5 — Indicative Offer / Letter of Intent (LOI)

The acquirer submits a non-binding **Letter of Intent** including:

| Element | Typical Content |
|---|---|
| Purchase Price | Indicative value, with +/- 10% range |
| Structure | Stock purchase, asset purchase, merger |
| Conditions | Financing, due diligence, regulatory approvals |
| Exclusivity | No-shop period for the seller (30–60 days) |

LOI negotiation creates alignment on headline economics before full due diligence.

---

### Step 6 — Due Diligence

Comprehensive investigation across workstreams.

| Workstream | Key Questions |
|---|---|
| **Financial DD** | Quality of earnings; working capital normalization; one-time items |
| **Legal DD** | Pending litigation, material contracts, change-of-control provisions |
| **Tax DD** | Tax attributes, NOLs, tax audits, transfer pricing |
| **Commercial DD** | Customer concentration, market share, pipeline strength |
| **Operational DD** | Systems fit, headcount plans, supply chain |
| **IT DD** | Cybersecurity, SaaS contracts, integration complexity |
| **HR/Personnel DD** | Bonus/option programs, employment agreements, benefit plan funding |

**Output**: DD report with deal-killers identified; Purchase Price Adjustment (PPA) basis.

---

### Step 7 — Definitive Agreement and Negotiation

Binding legal documentation:

- **Share Purchase Agreement (SPA)** or **Asset Purchase Agreement (APA)**
- Representations and Warranties (reps) from seller
- Indemnification and escrow provisions (typically 10–15% of price for 12–24 months)
- Closing Conditions (financing, regulatory, absence of MAC)
- **Disclosure Schedules**: exceptions to every representation

Finalize pricing and conditions; legal counsel reviews on both sides.

---

### Step 8 — Regulatory and Third-Party Approvals

Clearances required before closing:

- **Antitrust/competition**: HSR (US), European Commission, Competition Bureau (Canada)
- **Sector-specific**: FCC (media), CFIUS (US foreign investment), GDPR/data
- **Board and shareholder** approvals (corporate governance)

Target: no-action letter or clearance order received.

---

### Step 9 — Integration Planning

Begin planning **during** the LOI/exclusivity period so Day 1 readiness is achievable:

- **100-day plan** with top 10 integration priorities
- Synergy owner assignments (cost elimination targets, revenue capture)
- System and data integration (ERP, CRM, HRIS migrations)
- Cultural integration and communication cadence

Key principle: integration is executed **after** close but designed **before** close.

---

### Step 10 — Close and Post-Close Tracking

- Transfer funds (cash + stock mix per SPA)
- File regulatory forms; update public company disclosures if applicable
- Execute integration plan
- **Quarterly synergy tracking**: compare actual savings vs. plan
- **Holdback/escrow release** per indemnification schedule
- **Earnout** (if applicable): monitor earnout milestones and trailing financials

## Synergy Deep-Dive

Synergies justify the **control premium** paid over market price.

| Category | Examples | Realization Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Synergy** | Cross-sell to combined customer base; bundle pricing | 12–24 months |
| **Cost Synergy** | Headcount reduction; facility closure; procurement scale | 6–18 months |
| **Tax synergy** | Use NOLs; interest shield on acquisition debt; better transfer pricing | Immediate–ongoing |
| **Financial synergy** | Lower WACC via larger scale; improved credit rating | Ongoing |

**Formula**:

```
Total Value Created = Synergy Present Value − Acquisition Premium Paid
```

A transaction is accretive only when synergies exceed the premium paid over the pre-deal
market value of the target.

---

## Valuation in the M&A Context

In M&A, valuation models combine DCF with strategic methods:

- **DCF**: baseline intrinsic value for negotiation range
- **Comparable Companies**: market-multiple floor/ceiling
- **Precedent Transactions**: control premium benchmark

**Typical M&A premium**: 20–40% over pre-announcement market price.

## Deal Structure Comparison

| Structure | Description | Tax Treatment | Use Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stock Purchase** | Buyer buys target shares from shareholders | Target assets keep tax basis | Cleanest; assumes all liabilities |
| **Asset Purchase** | Buyer picks specific assets and liabilities | Step-up in basis → tax depreciation | Limited liability; carve-outs |
| **Merger** | Target merges into acquirer (or subsidiary) | Similar to stock purchase | Often requires shareholder vote |

## M&A Glossary (Key Terms)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Control Premium** | Extra price paid per share for control of the target (typically 20–40%) |
| **CIM (Confidential Information Memorandum)** | Confidential summary of a target for potential buyers |
| **DD (Due Diligence)** | Comprehensive pre-deal investigation |
| **Earnout** | Portion of price contingent on future performance targets |
| **Indemnification** | Seller's obligation to cover losses from breaches of reps |
| **LOI (Letter of Intent)** | Non-binding offer outlining deal terms |
| **MAC (Material Adverse Change)** | Clauses allowing exit if target's business deteriorates during exclusivity |
| **PPA (Purchase Price Allocation)** | Accounting allocation of purchase price to assets/liabilities acquired |
| **Reps and Warranties** | Promises by seller on business condition; backed by indemnification |
| **SPA (Share Purchase Agreement)** | Binding contract for purchase of company shares |
| **VDR (Virtual Data Room)** | Secure online repository for due diligence documents |
